Section 01

What the EU actually requires

The EU controls pet entry under Regulation (EU) No 576/2013 for non-commercial movement (owner traveling with pet), implemented by Regulation (EU) No 577/2013 which sets the official model health certificate format (Annex IV).

India is not listed in Annex II of EU Regulation 577/2013. This means your pet is treated as coming from an unlisted third country. The consequence is specific: your pet must pass a rabies antibody titer test done at an EU-approved laboratory, and you must wait at least 3 calendar months after the blood sample date before entering the EU.

📝
The Echinococcus (tapeworm) treatment that you may read about online applies to dogs only, under Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) No 1152/2011. It does not apply to cats. Both the regulation text and the EU Annex IV certificate confirm this explicitly — both options in Section II.4 state "the dogs described." Your vet must strike out the entire II.4 section for cats, initial it, and stamp.
🏥
DAHD empanelment is only required for the final EU Annex IV health certificate (the one submitted to AQCS for endorsement). Your microchip certificate, rabies vaccination record, and rabies titer result do not require a DAHD-empaneled vet. Any registered veterinarian can produce those documents.
📄
Different versions of the health certificate are allowed. The Danish Veterinary and Food Administration (FVST) confirmed: "Different versions of the Veterinary Certificate are allowed. However, it must be in compliance with Regulation (EU) 577/2013." Your vet can use a formatted version — the blank Annex IV Word template above is available for exactly this purpose.
Section 02

Step by step from scratch

1
Microchip your pet Must be first — before vaccination
ISO 11784/11785 compliant FDX-B transponder, 15-digit code. The chip must be implanted and verified readable before any rabies vaccination — vaccines given before microchipping are completely invalid in the EU. Get a written certificate from the vet confirming the chip number, implantation date, chip type, and location.
2
Rabies vaccination After microchip only
Any approved inactivated rabies vaccine. Record the vaccine name, manufacturer, batch/lot number, date of vaccination, validity from and to dates. The animal must be at least 12 weeks old at time of vaccination. At least 21 days must pass after vaccination before the titer blood draw.
3
Rabies antibody titer test EU-approved lab required
Blood sample must be taken at least 30 days after vaccination. Send to an EU-approved laboratory — APHA in the UK (lab.services@apha.gov.uk) is widely used and accepts samples from India. The result must show a neutralising antibody level of at least 0.5 IU/ml. A passing result is valid for the life of the animal as long as rabies boosters are kept current within their validity periods.
4
Wait 3 months from blood sample date Cannot be shortened
You cannot enter the EU until at least 3 calendar months have passed since the date the blood sample was taken for the titer test. This is the single longest constraint in the entire process and there is no way around it. Plan your travel date by counting forward 3 months from your blood draw date.
5
EU Annex IV animal health certificate Vet + AQCS endorsement
Valid for only 10 days from the vet's examination date to the date of your pet's entry at the EU border inspection post. An authorised veterinarian fills and signs it. All inapplicable clauses must be struck out and initialled by the vet. You submit to AQCS for endorsement and the certificate reference number. Download the template above to give to your vet.
6
AQCS submission and NOC 7 working days before travel
Submit your complete document set to AQCS online at least 7 working days before travel, then attend your in-person appointment. AQCS inspects all originals, endorses the Annex IV, and issues a No Objection Certificate (NOC) for export. Bring 2 sets of the Annex IV, 2 postcard photographs of the pet (4×6 inches, light facing), and all supporting documents.
7
Travel and EU customs clearance at first entry point
If your flight transits through any EU country before your final destination, that country is your first EU point of entry. You must clear customs there — not at your final destination. Go through the red channel with all original documents. Contact the veterinary border inspection post at your transit airport at least 2 weeks before travel to confirm the procedure and whether your layover time is sufficient.
Section 03

Every document you need

Carry all originals in hand luggage, never in checked baggage. Keep scanned copies on your phone as backup. Present originals at every checkpoint.

Core veterinary documents

Microchip certificate
From the implanting vet. Must show animal name, species, breed, sex, date of birth, microchip number (15-digit), chip type (ISO 11784/11785, FDX-B), date of implantation, implantation site, and confirmation chip was verified readable. Vet's name, license number, signature, and stamp. Any registered vet can issue this.
Rabies vaccination certificate
Vaccine name and manufacturer, batch/lot number, date of vaccination, validity from and to dates. Vet's name, license, signature, and stamp. Must explicitly confirm vaccination was administered after microchip implantation and that the chip was verified readable before vaccination.
Rabies antibody titer test result (FAVN test)
From an EU-approved laboratory. Must show animal identification, APHA reference number, date blood sample was taken, titer result in IU/ml, and pass/fail status. Minimum passing result is 0.5 IU/ml. Keep this document permanently — it is valid for the animal's life with boosters.
APHA UK: lab.services@apha.gov.uk | +44 208 415 2280
EU Annex IV Animal Health Certificate
Official EU model certificate under Regulation (EU) 576/2013. Issued and signed by authorised vet, endorsed by AQCS. Valid for 10 days from vet examination to EU entry. Certificate reference number assigned by AQCS. Inapplicable clauses struck out and initialled by vet. Submit 2 sets to AQCS — they retain one.
↓ Download the blank Word template at the top of this page
Veterinary Fitness to Fly certificate
A separate, simpler clinical certificate from any locally registered vet. Confirms the animal is free from signs of infectious disease and fit to travel by air on the specific travel date. Must include vaccine details, batch numbers, microchip number, and owner details. AQCS Bangalore retains this document at the appointment — bring the original.
Owner declaration (Annex IV Part 3)
A signed declaration by the owner that the animals are not being sold or transferred, will travel with the owner within 5 days, and will remain under the owner's responsibility. Must be attached to the Annex IV certificate. Must be in English and at least one official language of the EU entry country. Template included in the downloadable Word document above.
AQCS No Objection Certificate (NOC)
Issued by AQCS India at your in-person appointment after inspecting all documents. This is the official Government of India export clearance for your pets. AQCS will also provide attested copies of your documents.

Supporting documents for AQCS submission

  • Passport copy of all owners
  • Visa copy for destination country
  • Air ticket copy showing your travel date
  • 2 postcard size photographs of each pet, 4×6 inches, light facing the camera
  • Payment acknowledgement receipt (AQCS fee)
  • Pet registry certificates if available (e.g. TASSO for Germany — useful for EU recognition)
  • Airline pet booking confirmation (Lufthansa and most EU carriers require advance booking)
Section 04

Action timeline

Count backwards from your travel date. The 3-month wait after the titer blood draw is the fixed constraint that determines your earliest possible travel date.

Day −270 or earlier: Microchip implantation
Earliest sensible starting point to give yourself buffer time. Any registered vet can do this.
Same day or shortly after: Rabies vaccination
Must be after microchip implantation. Allow at least 30 days between vaccination and blood draw.
Day −239 or later: Blood draw for titer test
At least 30 days after vaccination. Send to EU-approved lab (APHA UK). Allow 2–3 weeks for results to arrive.
Day −90: Earliest possible EU entry date
3 calendar months must pass from the blood sample date. This is fixed and cannot be shortened under any circumstances.
Day −14: Book AQCS appointment
Contact your nearest AQCS office to book your in-person appointment. Confirm their current location — some offices have shifted premises.
Day −10: Earliest vet can sign Annex IV
Certificate is valid for only 10 days from signing to EU entry. Do not get it signed earlier than this date.
Day −7 (working days): AQCS online submission deadline
Submit full document set online to AQCS. This is 7 working days, not calendar days. Submit on Day −10 or earlier to be safe.
Day −3 to −1: AQCS in-person appointment
Bring all originals plus 2 sets of the Annex IV, 2 pet photographs, and all supporting documents. Allow a full day.
Day 0: Travel
All originals in hand luggage. At your first EU point of entry, proceed to the red channel at customs with all documents.
Section 05

AQCS India — what to expect

AQCS (Animal Quarantine and Certification Service) under the Department of Animal Husbandry and Dairying, Government of India, is the competent authority for pet export from India. There are AQCS offices at major international airports across India.

★★★★★

We recently completed the export process for our cats to Europe (Denmark) through AQCS Bangalore, and our experience was excellent from start to finish. The staff were extremely courteous, professional, responsive, and well organised. They guided us patiently through every step of the process and were always willing to clarify doubts over both phone and email.

For anyone traveling with pets to Europe, contact AQCS early and prepare your documents in advance. Keep your microchip certificate, rabies vaccination records, and rabies antibody titer test results ready according to the requirements of your destination country. After reviewing your documents, AQCS will provide an application form and declaration form that need to be completed.

For European countries, you will also require the EU Annex IV Animal Health Certificate. You can prepare this document using the template at the top of this page.

One important tip: obtain a Fit-to-Fly Certificate from your veterinarian within 10 days before travel and bring it with you to the AQCS appointment. AQCS will retain this document as part of the process. After verification, they issue the Government of India health certificate and provide the attested AQCS documents required for travel.

The process was efficient and well coordinated. A practical suggestion for visitors: AQCS Bangalore is located away from the city centre. Consider hiring a rental car for a round trip, or staying at one of the nearby resorts. The area is beautiful, peaceful, and pet friendly — a pleasant stop before international travel. On the way back, you will find a pet-friendly café, Beyond the Bean (Continental), which is well worth a visit.

Thank you to the entire AQCS Bangalore team for making what could have been a stressful process much smoother. Happy and safe travels to all pet parents flying with their companions.

Section 06

Transit through your first EU country

This is the step most people overlook. If your flight connects through any EU member state before your final destination — even briefly — that country is your first EU point of entry for customs and veterinary purposes.

For example, flying Bangalore → Munich → Copenhagen means Germany is your first EU point of entry, not Denmark. You must clear customs in Munich, not Copenhagen.

Per KBLV Munich (Bavarian veterinary border authority, bip-muc@kblv.bayern.de): pets traveling in cabin as hand luggage must proceed directly to customs via the red channel after passport control. Customs carries out the import control as an administrative obligation. Failure to comply is a punishable administrative offence. The process takes time — if your connection is under 3 hours, contact KBLV in advance to confirm it is sufficient.

Before you travel

Email the veterinary border inspection post at your transit airport at least 2 weeks before travel. Provide your flight details, the species and number of animals, and ask whether pre-notification is required and whether your connection time is sufficient. KBLV Munich can be reached at bip-muc@kblv.bayern.de or +49 89 97590390 (Monday to Friday, 08:00–16:30 German time).

Section 07

Arrival at your destination country

Even after clearing customs at your transit country, your destination country may require you to report at the Border Inspection Post on arrival. Confirm this with the destination country's veterinary authority before travel.

For Denmark, the relevant authority is FVST (Danish Veterinary and Food Administration) at fvst-spoergsmaal@fvst.dk. Notify them at least 24 hours before arrival. Copenhagen Airport (Kastrup), Billund Airport, and Aalborg Airport are the approved Border Inspection Posts for pet arrival in Denmark by air.

At Copenhagen, the Danish Customs Service will help ensure your pets are legally entered into the EU. Present all your original documents.

Section 08

Getting an EU Pet Passport after arrival

Once settled in your destination country, visit a local vet to get an EU Pet Passport issued. This is a physical booklet in the standardised EU format recording all identification, vaccination, and health information.

With an EU Pet Passport and current rabies boosters, your pet can move freely between all EU member states without repeating the import process. The passport is valid for the animal's life as long as vaccinations are kept current.

If you return to India and come back to the EU

Re-entering the EU from India triggers the full process again from India's side — new AQCS NOC, new Annex IV certificate. The EU Pet Passport does not cover re-entry from a third country. However, your original titer test result remains valid and does not need to be redone as long as rabies boosters were kept current without any lapse in validity.

Section 09

Common questions and mistakes

Does my cat need the Echinococcus tapeworm treatment?
No. Both options in Section II.4 of the EU Annex IV certificate explicitly state "the dogs described in Box I.28." Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) No 1152/2011, which mandates this treatment, applies only to dogs. For cats, the vet must strike out the entire II.4 section including both options and the treatment table, initial each strikeout, and stamp.
Does the titer test need to be repeated before each trip?
No. A passing titer test result is valid for the life of the animal, provided rabies booster vaccinations are administered within the validity period of each previous vaccination without any gap. Keep the original laboratory report permanently.
What exactly does "10 days validity" mean for the Annex IV certificate?
The certificate is valid for 10 days from the date the veterinarian signs and dates it until the date of the documentary and identity checks at the EU border inspection post. Count carefully from your vet examination date. A certificate signed even one day too early becomes invalid at entry.
Can any vet issue the Annex IV certificate?
The regulation allows it to be issued by an authorised veterinarian and subsequently endorsed by the competent authority (AQCS). The certificate reference number is assigned by AQCS. Confirm with your specific AQCS office whether your vet needs any additional authorisation in your state before they can issue the certificate.
My microchip was implanted after the rabies vaccination. What now?
That vaccination is invalid in the EU. You must implant a new compliant microchip, wait for it to be verified, then re-vaccinate, wait 30 days, do the titer blood draw, and wait 3 months again. This is the most common and most costly mistake in the entire process — always microchip before vaccination, without exception.
Can two cats travel on one Annex IV certificate?
Yes — Box I.28 has space for multiple animals. However if the animals have different owners, issuing separate certificates avoids confusion at border checks where officers may question why two different owners appear on one certificate.
Who strikes out the inapplicable clauses in the Annex IV?
The issuing veterinarian. Per Part 2 of the Annex IV explanatory notes: "statements which are not relevant may be crossed out and initialled and stamped by the official veterinarian, or completely deleted from the certificate." Deleting them before printing is the cleanest approach.
